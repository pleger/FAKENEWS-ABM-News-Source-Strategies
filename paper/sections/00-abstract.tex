\begin{abstract}
False-news sources can alter their presentation without changing publication veracity. We evaluate such strategies on X (formerly Twitter) with a survey-informed agent-based model of 500 synthetic users and four source objects. The staged design comprises 6,690 seeded runs across 423 conditions spanning imitation, campaign timing, direct reach, recommendation policy, and parameter and structural uncertainty. Combined imitation increased experimental-source decision share by 0.471 percentage points (pp) from a 1.328\% control mean, whereas its 0.157 pp false-decision-share effect---the false share among actual source-selection decisions---was imprecise. Short campaigns increased source share while active and produced a post-removal reversal. At 14.7\% direct reach, the combined false-decision-share effect was 0.149 pp under Perfect immediate (oracle), versus 9.097 pp under Fast imperfect and 15.131 pp under Delayed imperfect. Morris screening identified memory half-life as the largest influence on the combined strategy effect, but not an invariant leader. Audience gains remained positive across activity and topology cells, whereas false-exposure robustness depended on strategy and structure. Results are comparative outcomes under model assumptions, not forecasts of X.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} agent-based modelling; false news; misinformation; news sources; social media; sensitivity analysis
